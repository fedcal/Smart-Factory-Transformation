# Post-dyeing wash procedure and exhausted bath discharge

## Scope

This SOP describes the post-dyeing wash procedure (soaping, rinsing and finishing treatment) to be carried out in the **jet_dyeing** machine at the end of the dyeing cycle, before discharge of the exhausted **bagno_colorante** (dye bath) and unloading of the fabric. An effective post-dyeing wash removes unfixed dyestuff, residual chemical auxiliaries and hydrolysis products, guaranteeing the required **solidita_colore** and preventing **screziatura** (streakiness) from dyestuff migration during drying.

The procedure applies to dyeing of cellulosic fabrics (cotton, linen, viscose) with reactive dyestuffs, and synthetic fabrics with disperse dyestuffs. It does not apply to beam dyeing or continuous dyeing processes (pad-batch): those follow specific procedures.

Warning: the chemicals in the exhausted **bagno_colorante** (dyestuffs, auxiliaries, salts) require management in accordance with local environmental regulations before discharge to the industrial sewer.

## Prerequisites

- The main dyeing cycle is complete (process temperature reached and maintained for the time specified by the Recipe Sheet).
- The mid-cycle control sample (if specified) gave a conforming result.
- The site effluent treatment system is operational and available to receive the exhausted **bagno_colorante**.
- The technician has access to SDS sheets for the chemicals used and knows the emergency procedures for spills.

## Tools and PPE

- Calibrated digital pH meter (for discharge pH verification)
- Digital thermometer (for rinsing bath temperature verification)
- Sample collection container (250 mL, for pre-discharge bath analysis if required)
- Chemical-resistant gloves category III (nitrile)
- Waterproof apron
- Anti-splash safety glasses
- FFP2 half-face mask (for chemical vapours during high-temperature operations)

## Step-by-step Procedure

1. **Carry out the first hot rinse.** At the end of the dyeing cycle, discharge the main **bagno_colorante** (if the Recipe Sheet specifies discharge before soaping). Fill the **jet_dyeing** machine with hot water at 80 °C (same liquor ratio as the main cycle). Circulate the fabric for 10 minutes. Discharge. This rinse removes most of the unfixed dyestuff and salts.

2. **Carry out soaping.** Fill with water at 90-95 °C (for reactive dyestuffs) or at 70 °C (for disperse). Add soaping detergent (typically 1-2 g/L non-ionic detergent, as indicated by the Recipe Sheet). Circulate for 20 minutes at temperature. Soaping is critical for **solidita_colore**: it removes unfixed dyestuff that would otherwise transfer in use.

3. **Carry out the second hot rinse.** Discharge the soaping bath. Fill with hot water at 70-80 °C. Circulate 10 minutes. Discharge. This rinse eliminates residual detergent that could interfere with subsequent treatments.

4. **Carry out the final cold rinse.** Fill with water at ambient temperature (20-30 °C). Circulate 10 minutes. Measure the rinsing bath pH with the pH meter: it must be between 6 and 8 (indicative of complete neutralisation of alkaline or acid auxiliaries). If pH is out of range: carry out a second cold rinse.

5. **Add the finishing treatment (if specified by Recipe Sheet).** For some articles a finishing treatment is applied in the rinsing bath: cationic fixative (to improve reactive dyestuff wash fastness), softener (to modify fabric handle), or final acidification (to bring the substrate to the acid conservation pH). Add per the Recipe Sheet doses, circulate 15 minutes, then discharge.

6. **Verify the final exhausted bath pH before discharge.** With the pH meter, measure the pH of the bath about to be discharged to the sewer. The typical acceptable range for industrial sewer discharge is pH 6-9 (verify the site-specific limit with the environmental officer). If out of range: add acid (pH too high) or alkali (pH too low) and re-check.

7. **Unload the fabric.** Open the **jet_dyeing** machine door and unload the fabric into the collection trolley. Verify that the fabric is evenly distributed in the trolley (not tangled) to avoid **screziatura** from pressure during drying. Record on the production form: machine, lot number, recipe, end-of-cycle time, discharge pH, operator.

8. **Clean the jet dyeing machine.** After unloading the fabric: rinse the empty machine with cold water to remove dyestuff residues from the vessel, internal walls and nozzles. Inspect the nozzles for blockages (dyestuff or calcium residues): if blocked, proceed with cleaning per the specific maintenance procedure.

## Verification

- The final rinse pH (sample taken before discharge) is between 6 and 8.
- The fabric shows no visual **screziatura** on unloading (visual inspection in the collection trolley).
- The **solidita_colore** report carried out on a lot sample (SOP-DYE-004) confirms conforming fastness.
- The production form is completed with discharge pH, time and operator signature.
- The machine is clean and ready for the next cycle.

## Troubleshooting

**Final rinse pH does not fall below 9 after cold rinsing:**
- Add acetic acid (1-2 mL/L of 80% solution) to the final rinse bath and mix for 5 minutes. Re-measure pH. Do not add strong acid (hydrochloric, sulphuric) directly: risk of substrate damage.
- If the problem persists: verify that the preceding soaping did not contain an excess of alkalis; increase the intermediate hot rinse dose.

**Fabric shows visual streakiness (**screziatura**) on unloading (dark and light areas):**
- Pressure **screziatura** forms if the fabric piles up in the collection trolley at elevated temperatures (> 60 °C). In future: unload the trolley at a lower temperature or distribute fabric carefully. For the current lot: spread the fabric on a rack and dry in a laid-flat position for 30 minutes before completing drying.

**The jet dyeing nozzle is partially blocked (reduced circulation pressure):**
- Report immediately to the maintenance technician for nozzle cleaning before the next cycle. A blocked nozzle causes non-uniform bath distribution and structural **screziatura** in the next lot.
