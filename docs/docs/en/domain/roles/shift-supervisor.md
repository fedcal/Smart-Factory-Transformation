# Shift supervisor

The shift supervisor coordinates the entire production shift (8h), managing production priorities, operator allocation, maintenance technician interventions and communications with management. They are the Tier 2 escalation point for all anomalies that the operator cannot resolve autonomously. In an agentic system based on **hitl**, the shift supervisor is the primary referent for interruptions requiring human approval.

## Responsibilities

- Planning and assignment of operators to machines based on the daily production plan
- Real-time shift **oee** supervision: monitoring machine stops, intervention **mttr**, production progress vs plan
- Operational escalation management: decides whether to intervene with urgent corrective maintenance, stop a batch, or renegotiate priorities with planning
- Structured shift handover to the next shift supervisor: machine status, open anomalies, batches in progress, ordered spare parts
- Approval of Tier 2 **hitl** decisions generated by the agentic platform: unplanned maintenance orders, batch downgrades, line stops

## Typical interaction with assets and processes

The shift supervisor does not operate directly on machines but monitors all processes (**weaving**, **spinning**, **warping**, **dyeing**, **finishing**) via the supervision system. They interact with the quality manager for decisions on batches with **shade_deviation** or systematic **mispick**. They coordinate the maintenance technician for **warp_beam** changes and calibrations planned in the shift. They receive notifications from the agentic platform (via **nats** or dashboard) for anomalies that exceed the operator autonomy thresholds.

## Critical daily decision

The critical decision is: stop a **loom** or a **dyeing** plant in case of an anomaly that exceeds the autonomous intervention threshold. The shift supervisor assesses the impact on the delivery plan, the risk of progressive defects (e.g., **barring** on a batch in progress) and the availability of the technician for immediate intervention. They must balance: delivering the batch late vs delivering a defective batch, with different impacts on the customer and weekly **oee**.

## Pain points

- Shift handover — Handover between shifts is often verbal or on a paper log; the lack of a structured log of anomalies, stops and decisions causes loss of context and repetition of errors between successive shifts.
- **oee** not visible in real time — The shift supervisor has no access to a real-time **oee** dashboard per machine; shift performance estimation is based on manual piece count and comparison with the plan, with a 1-2 hour delay.
- **hitl** decision overload — When multiple machines simultaneously generate alerts and approval requests, the shift supervisor must manage a flow of interruptions that disrupts ordinary shift coordination, increasing the risk of hasty decisions on critical anomalies such as **warp_defect** or **shade_deviation**.

!!! note "Mantis context"
    Mantis has 3 shift supervisors alternating in the 3×8h cycle. The night shift (22-6h) operates with reduced supervision: the shift supervisor coordinates remotely on call for urgent stops. The morning handover (6h) is the most critical moment: batch priorities, batches with quality waivers and the status of ongoing maintenance interventions are shared.
